About Ferruh

I Found Coaching the Exact Moment I Needed It. Now I Help Others Find It Too.

Not as an escape from the pressure — as a way to finally lead it, instead of just surviving it.

For most of my career, I was the person everyone relied on to have it together.

By the time I was leading Operational Excellence for Toyota across Europe, I'd spent over a decade inside environments where the margin for error is zero — aviation safety, automotive manufacturing, medical devices. Regulated. Matrixed. High-stakes on a Tuesday afternoon. I was good at it. I delivered — millions in savings, faster lines, cleaner audits, teams that trusted me to hold the line when things got hard. From the outside, it looked exactly like success.

From the inside, something quieter was happening. The fire that used to fuel me had turned into discipline — I could still perform, I just wasn't sure anymore whether I actually wanted to, or whether I'd simply forgotten how to ask. I was leading projects across six production sites, sharp and effective at work — but I hadn't put nearly the same rigor into leading myself. I told myself it would ease up after the next project, the next reorg, the next milestone. It never quite did — because the thing that needed to change wasn't the workload. It was me.

Part of what made it harder to see was the pace of my own life. For years I lived as an expat, based between Europe and Japan for Toyota, travelling roughly 80% of the time across both. New cities, new teams, new time zones — it's easy to mistake constant motion for momentum. I was building a career I was genuinely proud of — I just hadn't been as deliberate about building the life around it.

Based between Europe and Japan, roughly 80% of the time on the move.

I found coaching at exactly the moment I needed it — not a moment before.

It didn't come as a grand epiphany. It came because I was finally honest enough to admit that being capable and being okay were not the same thing, and that no promotion, no title, and no amount of gritted-teeth discipline was going to close that gap for me. So I went looking for a way to actually work on it — not step away from my career to "find myself," because stepping away was never on the table. I needed something that worked inside the pressure, not instead of it.

What I found — and later trained in formally, becoming a Certified Life Coach — wasn't a set of tricks for managing stress. It was a way of seeing my own mind clearly enough to actually run it: to separate what was really happening from the story I was telling myself about it, to catch the thoughts that were quietly driving my exhaustion, and to choose, on purpose, who I wanted to be in the middle of the hardest weeks — instead of just reacting my way through them.

That's the part most people don't expect: the job didn't get easier. Philips is no less demanding than Toyota was. The regulated, high-stakes, always-on world I lead in every day hasn't changed at all. What changed is that I stopped needing my circumstances to change before I could feel like myself again. I got my command back — over my energy, my focus, my sense of who's actually driving — regardless of what was happening around me. That's the skill. It transfers to any circumstance, because it was never about the circumstance in the first place.

So I built the program I wish someone had handed me.

I founded what's now Re-Ignite for exactly one kind of person: someone who is genuinely good at their job, genuinely responsible for other people, and quietly out of gas in a way they've gotten very skilled at hiding. Not someone who needs to quit and reinvent their life. Someone who needs to reclaim command of it, in six weeks, without dropping a single thing they're actually responsible for.

I still carry a full leadership role today — Director, Senior Consultant at Philips, supporting Philips businesses and functions to up their game, whether that's safety, quality & regulatory, or productivity — because I built this from inside the exact pressure my clients are under, not from a distance. I'm not describing a path back to yourself. I'm still walking it, one demanding week at a time, and coaching from right there beside you.

Still leading from inside the room — Director, Senior Consultant, Philips.

I practice the same thing on the water that I teach in every session.

I'm a competitive sailor and certified skipper. Out there, conditions turn fast and you don't get to wait until you feel ready — you stay clear-headed and steady, whether you're at the helm or sailing as part of the crew, because people are counting on you either way. That's not a personality trait. It's a practice. It's the same practice, whether the storm is on the water or in a Monday morning inbox.

"I don't coach from the sidelines. I lead through it, right alongside you."
